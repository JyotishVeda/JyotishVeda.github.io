## What Simha actually is

Simha is the stretch of the sidereal zodiac from 120° – 150°, counted from the fixed
star reference point rather than from the spring equinox. That difference — the ayanamsha —
is currently a little under 24°, which is why a person called Leo by a Western
magazine is often a different rashi in Jyotish. The rashi that matters most in Indian
astrology is the one holding the **Moon** at birth, not the Sun.

Being the 5th rashi makes Simha a agni-element,
sthira-mode sign ruled by Surya (Sun). Those three facts —
element, mode and lord — carry most of the interpretive weight. Fixed fire sign of the Sun — authority, visibility and self-respect.

## The three structural facts

**Tattva: Agni (Fire).** The element describes the medium the sign works in.
Fire signs act before they deliberate, generate their own momentum and lose interest once the thing is started rather than finished.

**Swabhava: Sthira (Fixed).** Fixed signs hold things. They are associated with accumulation, persistence and resistance to being moved; the classical complaint is that they hold on past the point of usefulness.

**Lord: Surya (Sun).** A rashi is read through its ruler. Wherever Surya (Sun) sits in a chart,
and whatever condition it is in, is where the affairs of Simha are actually decided.
Surya signifies atma (soul), father, authority, vitality, bone and eyesight. It is exalted in Mesha 10° and
debilitated in Tula 10°, and runs a 6-year Vimshottari period.

## Traits the tradition associates with Simha

Classical texts describe the Simha native through the sign's emblem — lion —
and through its agni element. The recurring descriptions are
dignified, commanding, proud, generous and attention-seeking.

Read this carefully: these are descriptions of one factor in a chart, not of a person. A
Simha Moon in the 6th bhava with an afflicted Surya (Sun) looks nothing like a
Simha Moon in the 5th with Surya (Sun) exalted. The rashi supplies the flavour; the
bhava, the lord's condition and the running dasha supply the substance.

## The nakshatras inside Simha

Simha contains Magha, Purva Phalguni, Uttara Phalguni (pada 1). Because the nakshatra system divides the same circle
into 27 rather than 12, most rashis share a nakshatra with a neighbour. Where inside these
120° – 150° the Moon falls determines the janma nakshatra, the pada, and — crucially — which
graha's mahadasha the person is born into.

## Kalapurusha and the body

In the Kalapurusha scheme the twelve rashis map onto a single cosmic body from head to foot.
Simha, as rashi number 5, corresponds to **heart and upper back**. In
medical Jyotish this correspondence is read alongside the 6th bhava and the condition of the
rashi lord; it is a traditional framework and not a substitute for a doctor.

## Traditional associations

The day associated with Simha is Sunday, the day of its lord. The colour
group is gold, deep orange, the number is 1, and the stone
traditionally prescribed for strengthening Surya (Sun) is Ruby (Manik).

The beeja mantra for the rashi lord is *Om Suryaya Namah*, traditionally recited on
Sundays. Gemstone prescription in particular should not be done from a rashi page —
a stone strengthens a graha, and strengthening a graha that rules a difficult bhava for your
lagna is the opposite of helpful.
