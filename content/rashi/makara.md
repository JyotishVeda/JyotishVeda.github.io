## What Makara actually is

Makara is the stretch of the sidereal zodiac from 270° – 300°, counted from the fixed
star reference point rather than from the spring equinox. That difference — the ayanamsha —
is currently a little under 24°, which is why a person called Capricorn by a Western
magazine is often a different rashi in Jyotish. The rashi that matters most in Indian
astrology is the one holding the **Moon** at birth, not the Sun.

Being the 10th rashi makes Makara a prithvi-element,
chara-mode sign ruled by Shani (Saturn). Those three facts —
element, mode and lord — carry most of the interpretive weight. Movable earth sign of Saturn — structure, endurance and slow climb.

## The three structural facts

**Tattva: Prithvi (Earth).** The element describes the medium the sign works in.
Earth signs work in durable, material terms — what can be held, kept, measured or built on. Their weakness is inertia rather than haste.

**Swabhava: Chara (Movable).** Movable signs begin things. They are associated with travel, change of position and the initiating phase of any cycle; the classical complaint about them is that they rarely stay for the ending.

**Lord: Shani (Saturn).** A rashi is read through its ruler. Wherever Shani (Saturn) sits in a chart,
and whatever condition it is in, is where the affairs of Makara are actually decided.
Shani signifies longevity, discipline, labour, sorrow, servants, bones and nerves. It is exalted in Tula 20° and
debilitated in Mesha 20°, and runs a 19-year Vimshottari period.

## Traits the tradition associates with Makara

Classical texts describe the Makara native through the sign's emblem — crocodile / sea-creature —
and through its prithvi element. The recurring descriptions are
disciplined, ambitious, reserved, durable and duty-bound.

Read this carefully: these are descriptions of one factor in a chart, not of a person. A
Makara Moon in the 6th bhava with an afflicted Shani (Saturn) looks nothing like a
Makara Moon in the 5th with Shani (Saturn) exalted. The rashi supplies the flavour; the
bhava, the lord's condition and the running dasha supply the substance.

## The nakshatras inside Makara

Makara contains Uttara Ashadha (padas 2–4), Shravana, Dhanishta (padas 1–2). Because the nakshatra system divides the same circle
into 27 rather than 12, most rashis share a nakshatra with a neighbour. Where inside these
270° – 300° the Moon falls determines the janma nakshatra, the pada, and — crucially — which
graha's mahadasha the person is born into.

## Kalapurusha and the body

In the Kalapurusha scheme the twelve rashis map onto a single cosmic body from head to foot.
Makara, as rashi number 10, corresponds to **knees and joints**. In
medical Jyotish this correspondence is read alongside the 6th bhava and the condition of the
rashi lord; it is a traditional framework and not a substitute for a doctor.

## Traditional associations

The day associated with Makara is Saturday, the day of its lord. The colour
group is dark blue, black, the number is 8, and the stone
traditionally prescribed for strengthening Shani (Saturn) is Blue sapphire (Neelam).

The beeja mantra for the rashi lord is *Om Shanaischaraya Namah*, traditionally recited on
Saturdays. Gemstone prescription in particular should not be done from a rashi page —
a stone strengthens a graha, and strengthening a graha that rules a difficult bhava for your
lagna is the opposite of helpful.
