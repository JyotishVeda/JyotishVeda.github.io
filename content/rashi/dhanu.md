## What Dhanu actually is

Dhanu is the stretch of the sidereal zodiac from 240° – 270°, counted from the fixed
star reference point rather than from the spring equinox. That difference — the ayanamsha —
is currently a little over 24°, which is why a person called Sagittarius by a Western
magazine is often a different rashi in Jyotish. The rashi that matters most in Indian
astrology is the one holding the **Moon** at birth, not the Sun.

Being the 9th rashi makes Dhanu a agni-element,
dwisvabhava-mode sign ruled by Guru (Jupiter). Those three facts —
element, mode and lord — carry most of the interpretive weight. Dual fire sign of Jupiter — dharma, teaching and long-range aim.

## The three structural facts

**Tattva: Agni (Fire).** The element describes the medium the sign works in.
Fire signs act before they deliberate, generate their own momentum and lose interest once the thing is started rather than finished.

**Swabhava: Dwisvabhava (Dual).** Dual signs adapt and distribute. They sit between the fixed and movable phases, and are associated with mediation, teaching, transition and doing two things at once.

**Lord: Guru (Jupiter).** A rashi is read through its ruler. Wherever Guru (Jupiter) sits in a chart,
and whatever condition it is in, is where the affairs of Dhanu are actually decided.
Guru signifies wisdom, teachers, children, wealth, dharma, liver and fat. It is exalted in Karka 5° and
debilitated in Makara 5°, and runs a 16-year Vimshottari period.

## Traits the tradition associates with Dhanu

Classical texts describe the Dhanu native through the sign's emblem — archer —
and through its agni element. The recurring descriptions are
principled, expansive, teaching, restless and optimistic.

Read this carefully: these are descriptions of one factor in a chart, not of a person. A
Dhanu Moon in the 6th bhava with an afflicted Guru (Jupiter) looks nothing like a
Dhanu Moon in the 5th with Guru (Jupiter) exalted. The rashi supplies the flavour; the
bhava, the lord's condition and the running dasha supply the substance.

## The nakshatras inside Dhanu

Dhanu contains Mula, Purva Ashadha, Uttara Ashadha (pada 1). Because the nakshatra system divides the same circle
into 27 rather than 12, most rashis share a nakshatra with a neighbour. Where inside these
240° – 270° the Moon falls determines the janma nakshatra, the pada, and — crucially — which
graha's mahadasha the person is born into.

## Kalapurusha and the body

In the Kalapurusha scheme the twelve rashis map onto a single cosmic body from head to foot.
Dhanu, as rashi number 9, corresponds to **hips and thighs**. In
medical Jyotish this correspondence is read alongside the 6th bhava and the condition of the
rashi lord; it is a traditional framework and not a substitute for a doctor.

## Traditional associations

The day associated with Dhanu is Thursday, the day of its lord. The colour
group is yellow, saffron, the number is 3, and the stone
traditionally prescribed for strengthening Guru (Jupiter) is Yellow sapphire (Pukhraj).

The beeja mantra for the rashi lord is *Om Gurave Namah*, traditionally recited on
Thursdays. Gemstone prescription in particular should not be done from a rashi page —
a stone strengthens a graha, and strengthening a graha that rules a difficult bhava for your
lagna is the opposite of helpful.
