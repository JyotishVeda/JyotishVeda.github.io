## What Karka actually is

Karka is the stretch of the sidereal zodiac from 90° – 120°, counted from the fixed
star reference point rather than from the spring equinox. That difference — the ayanamsha —
is currently a little over 24°, which is why a person called Cancer by a Western
magazine is often a different rashi in Jyotish. The rashi that matters most in Indian
astrology is the one holding the **Moon** at birth, not the Sun.

Being the 4th rashi makes Karka a jala-element,
chara-mode sign ruled by Chandra (Moon). Those three facts —
element, mode and lord — carry most of the interpretive weight. Movable water sign of the Moon — memory, shelter and emotional tides.

## The three structural facts

**Tattva: Jala (Water).** The element describes the medium the sign works in.
Water signs work through absorption and retention. They register what happened and keep it, which is both their depth and their difficulty.

**Swabhava: Chara (Movable).** Movable signs begin things. They are associated with travel, change of position and the initiating phase of any cycle; the classical complaint about them is that they rarely stay for the ending.

**Lord: Chandra (Moon).** A rashi is read through its ruler. Wherever Chandra (Moon) sits in a chart,
and whatever condition it is in, is where the affairs of Karka are actually decided.
Chandra signifies manas (mind), mother, emotion, fluids, public and memory. It is exalted in Vrishabha 3° and
debilitated in Vrishchika 3°, and runs a 10-year Vimshottari period.

## Traits the tradition associates with Karka

Classical texts describe the Karka native through the sign's emblem — crab —
and through its jala element. The recurring descriptions are
nurturing, moody, protective, retentive and home-centred.

Read this carefully: these are descriptions of one factor in a chart, not of a person. A
Karka Moon in the 6th bhava with an afflicted Chandra (Moon) looks nothing like a
Karka Moon in the 5th with Chandra (Moon) exalted. The rashi supplies the flavour; the
bhava, the lord's condition and the running dasha supply the substance.

## The nakshatras inside Karka

Karka contains Punarvasu (pada 4), Pushya, Ashlesha. Because the nakshatra system divides the same circle
into 27 rather than 12, most rashis share a nakshatra with a neighbour. Where inside these
90° – 120° the Moon falls determines the janma nakshatra, the pada, and — crucially — which
graha's mahadasha the person is born into.

## Kalapurusha and the body

In the Kalapurusha scheme the twelve rashis map onto a single cosmic body from head to foot.
Karka, as rashi number 4, corresponds to **chest, lungs and stomach lining**. In
medical Jyotish this correspondence is read alongside the 6th bhava and the condition of the
rashi lord; it is a traditional framework and not a substitute for a doctor.

## Traditional associations

The day associated with Karka is Monday, the day of its lord. The colour
group is white, silver, the number is 2, and the stone
traditionally prescribed for strengthening Chandra (Moon) is Pearl (Moti).

The beeja mantra for the rashi lord is *Om Chandraya Namah*, traditionally recited on
Mondays. Gemstone prescription in particular should not be done from a rashi page —
a stone strengthens a graha, and strengthening a graha that rules a difficult bhava for your
lagna is the opposite of helpful.
