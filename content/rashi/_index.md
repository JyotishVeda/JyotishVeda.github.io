A rashi is a 30° division of the ecliptic. Twelve of them make the complete circle:
`360° ÷ 12 = 30°`. What separates the Indian system from the Western one is not the division
but the starting point. Jyotish measures from a fixed stellar reference; Western astrology
measures from the spring equinox, which drifts backwards by about 50 arcseconds a year. The
accumulated gap is the **ayanamsha**, currently a little under 24°.

That single offset is why people are so often a different sign here. It is also why the two
systems cannot be compared sign for sign — they are measuring different things from different
origins.

The second difference matters just as much: the rashi that Indian astrology treats as *your*
rashi is the one holding the **Moon** at birth — the janma rashi — not the Sun. If you have
never calculated it, the [Janma Rashi calculator](/tools/moon-rashi-calculator/) will work it
out from your birth details in the browser.
