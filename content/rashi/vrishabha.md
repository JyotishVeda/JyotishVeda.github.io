## What Vrishabha actually is

Vrishabha is the stretch of the sidereal zodiac from 30° – 60°, counted from the fixed
star reference point rather than from the spring equinox. That difference — the ayanamsha —
is currently a little under 24°, which is why a person called Taurus by a Western
magazine is often a different rashi in Jyotish. The rashi that matters most in Indian
astrology is the one holding the **Moon** at birth, not the Sun.

Being the 2nd rashi makes Vrishabha a prithvi-element,
sthira-mode sign ruled by Shukra (Venus). Those three facts —
element, mode and lord — carry most of the interpretive weight. Fixed earth sign of Venus — accumulation, comfort and staying power.

## The three structural facts

**Tattva: Prithvi (Earth).** The element describes the medium the sign works in.
Earth signs work in durable, material terms — what can be held, kept, measured or built on. Their weakness is inertia rather than haste.

**Swabhava: Sthira (Fixed).** Fixed signs hold things. They are associated with accumulation, persistence and resistance to being moved; the classical complaint is that they hold on past the point of usefulness.

**Lord: Shukra (Venus).** A rashi is read through its ruler. Wherever Shukra (Venus) sits in a chart,
and whatever condition it is in, is where the affairs of Vrishabha are actually decided.
Shukra signifies spouse, pleasure, art, vehicles, comfort, reproductive fluids. It is exalted in Meena 27° and
debilitated in Kanya 27°, and runs a 20-year Vimshottari period.

## Traits the tradition associates with Vrishabha

Classical texts describe the Vrishabha native through the sign's emblem — bull —
and through its prithvi element. The recurring descriptions are
steady, sensual, possessive, patient and resource-minded.

Read this carefully: these are descriptions of one factor in a chart, not of a person. A
Vrishabha Moon in the 6th bhava with an afflicted Shukra (Venus) looks nothing like a
Vrishabha Moon in the 5th with Shukra (Venus) exalted. The rashi supplies the flavour; the
bhava, the lord's condition and the running dasha supply the substance.

## The nakshatras inside Vrishabha

Vrishabha contains Krittika (padas 2–4), Rohini, Mrigashira (padas 1–2). Because the nakshatra system divides the same circle
into 27 rather than 12, most rashis share a nakshatra with a neighbour. Where inside these
30° – 60° the Moon falls determines the janma nakshatra, the pada, and — crucially — which
graha's mahadasha the person is born into.

## Kalapurusha and the body

In the Kalapurusha scheme the twelve rashis map onto a single cosmic body from head to foot.
Vrishabha, as rashi number 2, corresponds to **face, neck and throat**. In
medical Jyotish this correspondence is read alongside the 6th bhava and the condition of the
rashi lord; it is a traditional framework and not a substitute for a doctor.

## Traditional associations

The day associated with Vrishabha is Friday, the day of its lord. The colour
group is white, pastel blue, the number is 6, and the stone
traditionally prescribed for strengthening Shukra (Venus) is Diamond (Heera).

The beeja mantra for the rashi lord is *Om Shukraya Namah*, traditionally recited on
Fridays. Gemstone prescription in particular should not be done from a rashi page —
a stone strengthens a graha, and strengthening a graha that rules a difficult bhava for your
lagna is the opposite of helpful.
