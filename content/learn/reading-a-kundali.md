## Why order matters

Beginners usually start with whatever looks dramatic — a debilitated graha, a dosha, a
conjunction — and build a reading around it. The classical sequence goes the other way, from
the structural to the specific. Following it stops any single factor from dominating.

## 1. The lagna

Identify the rising rashi. It sets the entire house framework: which rashi falls in which
bhava, and therefore which grahas become functional benefics and malefics for this chart.
This is why the same graha is welcome in one chart and unwelcome in another.

## 2. The lagna lord

Find the ruler of the rising rashi and note its house, sign and dignity. A strong, well-placed
lagna lord supports everything else. This one placement often tells you more than any other.

## 3. The Moon

The Moon is the mind, and Indian astrology reads the chart a second time from the Moon as if
it were the lagna — the Chandra lagna. Where the two readings agree, the indication is strong.
The Moon's nakshatra also fixes the dasha sequence.

## 4. Dignity of every graha

Run through all nine: own sign, exaltation, debilitation, friendly or enemy sign, combustion
(too close to the Sun), retrograde motion. Note the outliers in both directions rather than
just the bad news.

## 5. Bhava lords and their placements

For each bhava you care about, find its lord and see where that lord sits. The 10th lord in
the 6th says something quite different from the 10th lord in the 10th. This step is where most
of the real information in a chart lives.

## 6. Yogas

Now look for named combinations — Raja yogas, Dhana yogas, Pancha Mahapurusha yogas and the
rest. Doing this last matters: a yoga formed by weak, badly placed grahas rarely delivers what
the name promises, and you can only tell that once you have done steps 2 to 5.

## 7. The running dasha

Finally, check which mahadasha and antardasha are running. The chart shows potential; the
dasha decides timing. A strong yoga involving a graha whose period comes at 62 is a very
different life from the same yoga activating at 22.

## A closing caution

A chart is a traditional interpretive object. Read carefully it can be a useful frame for
reflection; read carelessly it becomes a way of handing your decisions to a diagram. Nothing
in a kundali should substitute for medical, legal, financial or psychological advice, and no
chart determines anyone's worth or future.
