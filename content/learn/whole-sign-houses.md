## The rule

In whole-sign houses the rashi rising at birth becomes the entire 1st bhava. The next rashi is
the entire 2nd, the one after that the entire 3rd, and so on around the circle.

If Kanya rises at 3° or at 28°, Kanya is the whole 1st bhava either way. A graha at 27° Kanya
is in the 1st house, not "almost in the 2nd".

## Why this simplifies everything

Western astrology has a long-running dispute about house division — Placidus, Koch, Campanus,
Regiomontanus, Equal and others, each producing different cusps and therefore different house
placements for the same chart. The quadrant systems also misbehave at high latitudes, where
some houses become enormous and others vanish.

Whole-sign has none of these problems. There are no cusps to compute, no latitude failure
modes, and no ambiguity about which house a graha occupies. Rashi and bhava are the same
twelve divisions, counted from a different starting point.

## What about the Midheaven?

In a quadrant system the Midheaven is the 10th house cusp by definition. Under whole-sign it
is simply a sensitive degree that falls wherever it falls — often in the 9th, 10th or 11th
bhava. Indian practice treats the 10th bhava as the house of karma regardless of where the MC
lands, and reads the MC, when at all, as a separate point.

## Bhava Chalit

Indian astrology does have a cusp-based chart: the **Bhava Chalit**, which uses house
midpoints and can place a graha in a different bhava than the rashi chart does. Most
practitioners read the rashi chart as primary and consult the Chalit as a refinement for
grahas sitting near a sign boundary.

This site uses whole-sign throughout, which is the standard both for the North Indian diamond
and the South Indian grid, and the convention nearly every classical text assumes.
