## What each one measures

The **tropical** zodiac divides the year by the Sun's relationship to the Earth's equator. Its
0° is the March equinox. It is, in effect, a calendar of seasons: 0° Aries is the start of
northern spring by definition, and stays there permanently.

The **sidereal** zodiac divides the sky by the fixed stars. Its 0° is anchored to a stellar
reference. It is, in effect, a map of where the constellations are.

Both are internally consistent. Both describe something real. They describe different things,
and the [ayanamsha](/learn/ayanamsha-explained/) is the distance between them.

## The argument each side makes

Defenders of the tropical zodiac point out that the signs were always defined by the seasonal
quarters — the equinoxes and solstices — and that the constellation names were labels rather
than definitions. On this view, drift is irrelevant because the constellations were never the
referent.

Defenders of the sidereal zodiac point out that Indian astronomy has always worked from the
nakshatras, which are actual star groups, and that a system anchored to stars should stay
anchored to stars. The nakshatra framework predates the twelve-sign framework in Indian
sources, and it is unambiguously stellar.

Both arguments are coherent on their own terms. There is no measurement that settles the
question, because the two systems are not making competing claims about the same quantity.

## Where this leaves a reader

A few things are worth being clear about.

Neither zodiac has demonstrated predictive validity in controlled studies. The disagreement
between them is a disagreement inside a traditional interpretive practice, not a scientific
dispute with an experimental resolution.

Comparing your charts across systems is interesting but the two are not commensurable. A
sidereal Meena Moon and a tropical Aries Moon can be the same Moon described from two origins
— "which is the real one" has no answer that both systems would accept.

And the sign — in either system — is a small part of a reading. A Jyotish chart weighs the
lagna, the bhava a graha occupies, its dignity, the aspects onto it and the running dasha. The
rashi label is where a reading begins, not where it ends.

## What this site does

JyotishVeda is sidereal throughout, using the Lahiri ayanamsha, because that is the standard of
the tradition it documents. The ayanamsha applied is printed on every calculated result, so
the tropical value is always one subtraction away.
