## The system

Ashtakoota — "eight groupings" — compares the Moon's rashi and nakshatra in two charts and
awards up to 36 points. It is the most widely used matching method in North India. South
Indian practice more often uses a ten-koota system (Dasakoota) and weighs the horoscopes
differently.

Six of the eight kootas depend only on the two nakshatras or the two rashis, which is why a
match can be scored knowing nothing else about either chart.

## What each koota is testing

**Varna (1).** Compares the traditional social-function grouping of the two rashis. Scores if
the boy's varna is not below the girl's.

**Vashya (2).** Groups rashis by type — quadruped, human, water-dwelling, wild, insect — and
scores mutual ease.

**Tara (3).** Counts nakshatra distance in both directions. Remainders of 3, 5 and 7 after
division by 9 mark the inauspicious taras: Vipat, Pratyari and Vadha.

**Yoni (4).** Each nakshatra carries an animal symbol with a gender. Identical animals score
full marks, the seven sworn-enemy pairs score zero.

**Graha Maitri (5).** Compares the two rashi lords using the standard table of natural
planetary friendship. This is the koota closest to what a modern reader would call
temperamental compatibility.

**Gana (6).** Deva, Manushya or Rakshasa temperament. The table is asymmetric: a Rakshasa-gana
boy with a Deva or Manushya girl scores zero, while the reverse combinations score better.

**Bhakoot (7).** Rashi distance. The 2/12, 5/9 and 6/8 axes score zero and are named as
doshas in their own right.

**Nadi (8).** Adi, Madhya or Antya — an Ayurvedic constitutional grouping. Identical nadi
scores zero, which is why this single koota can sink an otherwise strong total.

## Thresholds

Eighteen of 36 is the conventional minimum. Twenty-four and above is a good match; above
thirty, excellent. Below eighteen the pairing is traditionally rejected — though "rejected"
in practice means "examined further", since the exemptions are extensive.

## The honest limits

Ashtakoota compares two Moons. It does not look at the lagna, the 7th bhava, the 7th lord,
Venus, Mars, longevity indicators or the dasha periods either person is running. A careful
astrologer treats the score as a first filter and then reads both full charts — and will
sometimes clear a 16-point match while declining a 30-point one.

It is also worth saying plainly: no koota table has been shown to predict marital outcomes.
Guna Milan is a structured traditional practice with deep cultural roots. It is not a
compatibility test in the empirical sense, and it should never be the reason two people who
want to marry are prevented from doing so.
