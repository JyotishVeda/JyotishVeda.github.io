## The 1st bhava in one paragraph

The Tanu Bhava — House of the Body — covers physical body, appearance, temperament, vitality, early life, the self as it is seen. It is classified as
**Kendra + Trikona (Lagna)**, its natural karaka is **Surya**, and it belongs to the
**Dharma** group of life aims.

The rising sign — body, personality and the frame of the whole chart.

## How the bhava is actually read

There are four questions, asked in order:

1. **Which rashi falls here?** On this site houses are whole-sign, so the rashi and the bhava
   are the same thing. If Mesha rises, Mesha is the entire 1st bhava, Vrishabha the entire
   2nd, and so on.
2. **Where is the lord of that rashi?** The lord's own placement usually matters more than
   anything sitting in the bhava. A 1th lord in a kendra or trikona supports the
   affairs of this house wherever it sits.
3. **What occupies the bhava, and what aspects it?** Occupants colour the house directly;
   aspects modify it from a distance.
4. **What does the karaka say?** For the 1st bhava that is Surya.
   A house and its karaka should agree before you commit to a reading.

## Classification: Kendra + Trikona (Lagna)

**Kendra** (angular — houses 1, 4, 7, 10) are the pillars of the chart. Grahas here act visibly and with force. The classical quirk is that natural benefics gain and natural malefics lose some of their ability to harm from a kendra.

**Trikona** (trinal — houses 1, 5, 9) are the fortunate houses, associated with dharma, merit and support. No graha is considered to do badly from a trikona.

## Purushartha: Dharma

The twelve bhavas fall into four groups of three, matching the four aims of life — dharma
(purpose), artha (resources), kama (desire) and moksha (release). The
1st belongs to **Dharma**, which puts it in the same group as
the 5th, 9th.
Houses in one group tend to be read together.

## Body correspondence

The 1st bhava corresponds to head, complexion, overall constitution. Medical Jyotish reads this
alongside the 6th bhava, the 8th for chronic matters and the condition of the relevant karaka.
This is a traditional framework for study, not a diagnostic method.

## When this bhava is considered strong

Its lord is strong, in a kendra or trikona, and the lagna is not afflicted by malefics.

Strength is never read from a single indicator. A bhava with a well-placed lord, a benefic
occupant and a supportive dasha behaves quite differently from the same bhava with the lord in
a dusthana, however promising the occupants look.
