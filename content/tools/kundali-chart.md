## What the chart contains

The **lagna** is the sidereal rashi rising on the eastern horizon at your birth instant and
place. It is computed from local sidereal time and geographic latitude, and it moves about one
degree every four minutes — which is why the birth time matters more here than anywhere else
in the chart.

The nine **grahas** are placed by sidereal longitude. Tropical positions come from an
astronomical ephemeris; the Lahiri ayanamsha is then subtracted to give the sidereal value.
Rahu and Ketu are the Moon's mean nodes, always 180° apart and always retrograde.

**Bhavas** are whole-sign: the rising rashi is the entire 1st house, the next rashi the entire
2nd, and so on. This is the standard Indian convention and avoids the house-system disputes
that occupy Western astrology.

## North Indian and South Indian styles

Both charts show the same data arranged differently.

In the **North Indian** diamond the twelve house positions are fixed on the page — the top
centre diamond is always the 1st house — and the rashi numbers move. In the **South Indian**
square grid the twelve rashis are fixed — Mesha always sits in the same cell — and the lagna
is marked with "La". Neither is more accurate; use whichever you were taught.

Retrograde grahas are marked with a superscript R.

## Reading the dasha table

The Vimshottari sequence below the chart begins with the lord of the Moon's birth nakshatra.
The first period is partial: whatever fraction of the nakshatra the Moon had already crossed
is the fraction of that lord's period already spent before birth. The
[dasha article](/learn/vimshottari-dasha-maths/) works through the arithmetic.
