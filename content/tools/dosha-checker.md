## Mangal dosha

Mars is counted as afflicting when it occupies the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house from
a reference point. Which reference point? That is precisely where traditions differ, so this
tool checks all three that are commonly used — the Lagna, the Moon and Venus — and reports
each separately rather than collapsing them into one verdict.

Several widely accepted cancellations exist: Mars in its own or exaltation sign, Mars with or
aspected by Jupiter, particular lagnas, and the case where both charts carry the dosha. The
tool lists the ones it detects.

The honest summary: a large share of charts show Mars in one of those houses from at least one
reference point. A flag here is a prompt to look closer, not a verdict on anyone's marriage.

## Sade Sati

Sade Sati is the roughly seven-and-a-half-year span in which Saturn transits the 12th, 1st and
2nd rashis from the natal Moon — two and a half years in each. It recurs about every 29 to 30
years, so most people meet it two or three times in a life.

The tool also checks the two **dhaiyas**: Kantaka Shani (Saturn in the 4th from the Moon) and
Ashtama Shani (Saturn in the 8th), each lasting about two and a half years.

Traditionally Sade Sati is described as a period of pressure, responsibility and slow
consolidation rather than disaster. It is worth noting that it depends only on your Moon's
rashi and the current date, so roughly one-eighth of the world is in it at any moment.

## Kaal Sarp Yoga

The classical condition is that all seven non-nodal grahas fall on one side of the Rahu–Ketu
axis. This tool requires all seven — no partial or "broken" variants, which different schools
count differently.

Kaal Sarp is a comparatively late addition to the literature and is not found in the oldest
classical texts. It attracts a large remedial industry. Treat a positive result as a point of
study rather than a reason for expensive intervention.
