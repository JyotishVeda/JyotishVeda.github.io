## Why your Vedic rashi differs from your Western sign

Two separate differences stack up.

**The body.** Western sun-sign columns use the Sun. Indian astrology uses the Moon. Those are
different bodies in different places.

**The zodiac.** Western astrology measures from the spring equinox, which drifts backwards
about 50 arcseconds each year. Indian astrology measures from a fixed stellar reference. The
gap between the two — the ayanamsha — is currently a little over 24°, roughly four-fifths of
a rashi.

Between them, the two differences mean your janma rashi is usually not the sign you grew up
calling yours. The tool shows the tropical longitude, the ayanamsha it subtracted and the
resulting sidereal longitude, so you can follow the conversion rather than take it on trust.

## Birth time

The Moon covers about 13° a day, so it changes rashi roughly every two and a quarter days. If
you were born near a boundary, an hour of error can move the answer. Where the birth time is
genuinely unknown, noon is the conventional placeholder — but treat a boundary result as
uncertain rather than settled.
