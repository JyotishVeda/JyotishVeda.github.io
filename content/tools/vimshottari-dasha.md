## The 120-year cycle

Vimshottari is the main timing system of Vedic astrology. Nine grahas hold periods that add
to exactly 120 years:

| Graha | Years | Graha | Years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ketu | 7 | Rahu | 18 |
| Shukra | 20 | Guru | 16 |
| Surya | 6 | Shani | 19 |
| Chandra | 10 | Budha | 17 |
| Mangal | 7 | **Total** | **120** |

The sequence always runs in that order and always begins with the lord of the Moon's birth
nakshatra.

## Balance at birth

The first mahadasha is almost never complete. Whatever fraction of the nakshatra the Moon had
already crossed at your birth is the fraction of that lord's period already spent. If the Moon
had covered 40% of a nakshatra ruled by a graha with a 20-year period, 8 years are gone and
12 remain.

The tool prints the fraction it measured and the resulting balance, so the figure is checkable.

## Antardashas

Each mahadasha subdivides into nine antardashas in the same order, proportioned by the same
year-counts: an antardasha lasts `(mahadasha years × antardasha lord's years) ÷ 120`. The
running mahadasha's antardasha grid is shown below the main sequence.

## Reading the dasha honestly

A dasha tells you *when* a graha delivers. What it delivers depends on that graha's house,
sign, dignity and aspects in your own chart. The same Shani mahadasha is a period of
consolidation for one person and of heavy obligation for another — the period name alone
decides nothing.
