*Panchang* means "five limbs". The Hindu calendar is built from five quantities, and all five
come from just two numbers: the sidereal longitudes of the Sun and the Moon.

| Limb | Definition | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vara | The weekday, counted from sunrise | 7 |
| Tithi | `floor((Moon − Sun) ÷ 12°) + 1` | 30 |
| Nakshatra | The Moon's nakshatra | 27 |
| Yoga | `floor((Moon + Sun) ÷ 13°20′) + 1` | 27 |
| Karana | Half a tithi — 6° of elongation | 60 half-tithis, 11 names |

A tithi is not a day. It is the time the Moon takes to gain 12° on the Sun, which varies from
roughly 19 to 26 hours depending on where both bodies are in their orbits. That is why tithis
sometimes skip a civil day and sometimes repeat one.

The panchang above is computed for 06:00 local time at the selected place, which is the
conventional approximation used when exact local sunrise is not being calculated.
